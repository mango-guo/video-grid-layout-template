Kitchen frame · 1920×1080 text-layout template

1Text sits where the footage is quiet

The headline block uses columns 1–4 over the dark wall (zone A). The window, the device and the couple each stay clear of text. Book p. 97: plan the image together with where the type will go.

2Three clearly different sizes

Eyebrow 24 / headline 72 on 84 / body 32 on 48. Each step is a visible jump, so the levels don't read as one (p. 45).

3One shared left edge

Eyebrow, headline and body all hang from x = 120 (column 1). Lining up on one edge tells the viewer they are one message (pp. 10–13).

4Spacing in 12-px steps

Gaps are 24, 36 and 48 px. The rhythm is set by these steps, not by eye, per shot.

5Label tied to its object

The UI label starts on column 6 and a leader line runs to the screen. A data label must stay visibly attached to the thing it describes.

6Subtitles have their own zone

Chinese above English, centred over columns 3–10, sitting on the 96-px bottom margin. They are one group, kept apart from the headline system.

7Space left empty on purpose

Columns 7–12 carry no type. The embrace is the message, and the open space lets it land (p. 72).

8Timing (suggested)

Headline in at 0.0–0.6 s, body +0.3 s, UI label at about 1.5 s once the eye has found the device, subtitles synced to the VO. Hold everything at least 2.5 s after the last element settles.

Sizes are 1920×1080 design pixels. The headline, the UI label and the subtitles each need a contrast check on the real footage. The UI label sits over a blurred mid-tone table, so add a soft 20–30% scrim if it reads weakly. Not yet checked at phone size.
